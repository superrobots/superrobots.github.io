Joyson delivers its first robot compute backpack to Unitree

The add-on combines a computer and power system so robots can process sensor data and run AI models locally.

Delivered 10 Nov 2026·Company announcement·English brief

Joyson Electronics says its robotics subsidiary delivered a first compute backpack to Unitree on November 10. The external module includes a local computing platform and its own power system. Joyson says the design can work with Unitree's humanoid, four-legged, and wheeled robots.

The idea is to give a robot more room to process sensor inputs and run AI models on board. Joyson also says the companies are working on pilots for auto-parts production and other manufacturing tasks.

The announcement describes a first delivery. It gives no measured improvement in battery life or timetable for broader availability.
